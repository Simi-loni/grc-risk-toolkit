# Risk scoring methodology

This is a simple 5 x 5 method. Every number below is a choice a company makes for itself. Change them to match the company's own risk policy.

## Scales

| Score | Likelihood | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rare: unlikely in the next 12 months | Minor: no customer effect, fixed in a day |
| 2 | Unlikely: could happen once in 2 to 3 years | Low: small customer effect, no data affected |
| 3 | Possible: could happen in the next 12 months | Moderate: some customers affected, or a contract or policy breach |
| 4 | Likely: expected in the next 12 months | Major: significant customer data or service effect |
| 5 | Almost certain: already happening or expected within months | Severe: large-scale data exposure, extended outage, or regulatory action |

## Score and rating

Score = likelihood x impact (1 to 25).

| Score | Rating |
|---|---|
| 1 to 4 | Low |
| 5 to 9 | Medium |
| 10 to 14 | High |
| 15 to 25 | Critical |

## Risk tolerance (example)

- Risks scored 10 or above are above tolerance and need a treatment plan with an owner and a due date.
- A risk above tolerance can only be accepted if someone in leadership signs it off by name.
- Risks scored below 10 can be accepted by the risk owner.

## Treatment options

- **Mitigate:** reduce likelihood or impact with a control or change.
- **Transfer:** share the risk, for example through insurance or a contract.
- **Avoid:** stop the activity that creates the risk.
- **Accept:** knowingly live with the risk, with sign-off as above.
